# 08 — Compliance and content restrictions

> Status: **stub** — outline only.

**Question this document answers:** what rules constrain what can be shown on a
given panel, and how does an agent learn them before it books?

## What this document will cover

- [ ] Restrictions by law and regulation: tobacco, alcohol, gambling, pharma,
      political advertising, financial promotions, age-restricted categories
- [ ] Geographic specificity of those rules: national, regional and municipal
- [ ] Proximity rules: distance from schools, places of worship, clinics
- [ ] Landlord and site-owner restrictions, which are contractual and per-site
- [ ] Transit and airport operator rules
- [ ] Category exclusivity and competitive separation within a loop or a location
- [ ] Public-space content standards: nudity, shock, language, political content
- [ ] Clearance as a gate before scheduling, and revocation mid-campaign
- [ ] Who is liable when an agent books something non-compliant

## Why the digital-first assumption breaks

Digital brand-suitability models filter *content the ad appears next to*. OOH needs
the inverse: rules attached to the *physical location* that constrain the ad itself,
plus a hard clearance gate before anything can run. A public screen is also seen by
non-consenting passers-by, including minors, which raises the bar above what a
private feed requires.
